## What I learned
- I learned more about the difference between a **Business Problem (BP)** and an **Analytics Problem (AP)**. The Business Problem is the broader issue the organization wants to solve, while the Analytics Problems are the specific questions that data can help answer.

- This was harder to apply than I expected. A lot of us confused the two during the exercises, but Ms. Michelle was very helpful in correcting our answers and explaining the distinction.

- This is where the **fail fast** mindset really stood out to me. It is better to try, get corrected, and improve my thinking early than to avoid answering because I am afraid of being wrong.

- I also finally understood the difference between **accuracy** and **consistency** more clearly. Consistency is about whether data agrees across sources, while accuracy requires a true or correct value that we can compare against.

- I was happy that I got to recite when we were asked what happens if data is left unchecked. I was able to connect the discussion with what I learned from the Coursera course I have been taking, which made the extra studying feel worth it.

- Another important takeaway was the responsible use of data. Good analysis is not only about getting the numbers right, but also about considering data quality, privacy, bias, transparency, and the impact of the decisions made from it.

## Terms I am still learning
- **Business Problem (BP)** - the main organizational issue or objective that needs to be addressed.

- **Analytics Problem (AP)** - a specific analytical question derived from the Business Problem that data can help answer.

- **Problem Framing** - the process of clearly defining the problem before deciding what data or solution is needed.

## What confused me
- I am still slightly confused when distinguishing Business Problems from Analytics Problems in actual scenarios. The concept makes sense, but applying it correctly still takes practice.

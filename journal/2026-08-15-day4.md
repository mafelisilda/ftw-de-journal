## Today in one sentence

## What I learned
- In the morning, we presented our group homework in Databricks. It was nice to present something we had built ourselves and explain how we approached the data.

- I liked the analogy between the **water lifecycle and the data lifecycle**. Just as water goes through different stages before it becomes usable, data also needs to be collected, processed, stored, and served before it becomes useful to its users.

- We were introduced to the **four Data Engineering lenses** for classifying data: role, structure, change, and sensitivity. My groupmates and I found this surprisingly challenging because the classification can depend on the context.

- I was able to recite about the difference between `CHAR` and `VARCHAR`, which made me happy because I have been taking several DataCamp courses related to SQL. It is nice to see the extra practice paying off during class.

- And finally: **hello, pipelines!** We started looking at how work that was previously done as a one-time analysis can become a repeatable process.

## Terms I am still learning
- **Data Lake** - a flexible storage approach that can hold different types of raw data.

- **Data Lakehouse** - an architecture that combines characteristics of data lakes and data warehouses.

- **Delta Lake** - a technology used with data lakes that adds features such as ACID transactions, versioning, time travel, and schema enforcement.

## What confused me
- I am still confused about the differences between a **Data Lake, Data Lakehouse, and Delta Lake**. The names are very similar, so I need to understand their respective roles more clearly.

- Classifying data using the four DE lenses was also harder than I expected, especially because the same piece of data may be classified differently depending on its context.
